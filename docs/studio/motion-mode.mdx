# Motion — Animating Your Key Visual

**Motion** turns a finished Key Visual into a short video. Instead of describing the movement and hoping, you animate the KV's own layers on a timeline: pick a layer, apply a motion preset or set keyframes yourself, scrub the playhead, add audio, and export an MP4.

> **Experimental feature:** Motion is a Labs feature enabled per workspace. If the **Motion** button isn't in your KV file's bottom toolbar, it isn't turned on yet — see [Turning On Experimental Features](../settings/experimental-features).

> **Changed from the old Motion:** Motion used to be a single "describe it and generate" panel. It's now a timeline editor with its own projects, so your work is saved and editable rather than being one-shot output.

## Before You Start

1. ✅ **A finished board in your KV file** — Motion animates the board you're viewing
2. ✅ **Motion enabled for your workspace** — see the note above
3. ✅ **A brand-scoped KV file** — a file not attached to a brand can't open Motion

## Opening Motion

1. Open a KV file in [KV Generator](./kv-generator)
2. In Craft, click the **Motion** button (film icon) in the bottom toolbar
3. The **Motion projects** panel opens, marked **Experimental**

A **motion project** is one timeline document. A KV file can hold several — different cuts, different sizes — each listed with its name and when it was last updated.

If the list is empty it says so: _"No motion projects yet. Animate the board you're viewing with a keyframed timeline."_

> **Heads-up:** **New motion** is disabled until there's a board to animate. The tooltip explains why — _"Generate a board first — Motion animates the board you're viewing."_

## Creating a Motion Project

Click **New motion**. A picker opens headed **Choose the video size**, described as _"Motion animates the picture shown in the preview."_

Sizes are grouped:

| Group | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **Same as board** | Your board at its own dimensions |
| **From your resizes** | Sizes you already produced in the [Resizer](./resizer) |
| Platform groups | The standard platform sizes |

Tick one size or several, then click **Create motion · N sizes**. The footer shows the **Cost** and your **Balance** before you start; creating a motion has a fixed price per size. If you can't cover it, the button is disabled and the picker says _"Not enough credits — top up to create this motion."_

> **Tip:** a size that already exists as a resize carries that resize's own layout, so the motion matches the artwork you actually approved for that platform.

## The Editor

| Area | What's there |
|---|---|
| **Top bar** | **Back**, project name, preset + **Stagger**, **Animate all**, the focused tile's size, **Export**, exported files, save state, **⋮** |
| **Layers** (left) | One row per layer in your KV, plus a locked **Base image** row |
| **Properties** (right) | The selected keyframe's values, and the canvas zoom controls |
| **Canvas** | Live preview — one stage per output size |
| **Timeline dock** (bottom) | The playhead, tracks, and transport controls |

Your work saves by itself. The top bar shows **Saved**, **Saving…**, or **Failed to save**.

### Layers

Each layer from your KV appears as a track — text, image, or shape — named after its content. The **Base image** row is locked: it's the artwork underneath, and it doesn't carry keyframes.

Click a layer to select it. The **✨** button on the row applies a motion preset to it.

## Motion Presets

Rather than keyframing everything by hand, apply a preset. The **Default** tab of the **✨** menu holds a library of 50 presets in four sections:

| Section | Examples | Where it plays |
|---|---|---|
| **Entrance** | Fade in, Slide in, Rise, Pop in, Zoom in, Stomp, Bounce in, Drop in, Rotate in, Jack-in-the-box | Brings the layer in |
| **Exit** | Fade out, Slide out, Rise out, Zoom out, Pop out, Bounce out, Fall | Takes the layer out at the end |
| **Emphasis** | Pulse, Shake, Spin, Bounce, Heartbeat, Tada, Swing, Wobble, Flash, Hop | A short highlight at the playhead |
| **Loop** | Float, Breathe, Sway, Twinkle, Turn | Repeats between the layer's entrance and exit |

Presets that only differ by direction share one card. **Slide in**, for example, reads **Slide in · 4 directions**, and you pick the direction in the Tune panel.

> **Heads-up:** emphasis presets are disabled when the playhead is too close to the end: _"Not enough room before the end."_ Loop presets need at least 0.4 seconds free before the layer's exit or its next motion, and say so when there isn't room.

### Tuning a preset

After you pick a preset, the menu stays open and a **Tune** panel appears at the top, so you can adjust the motion without starting over:

| Row | Options |
|---|---|
| **Direction** | The preset's directions, for example Rise, Sink, Drift left or Drift right |
| **Distance** / **Intensity** | **Subtle**, **Normal** or **Big**: how far it travels, or how strong the effect is |
| **Duration** | How long it lasts |
| **Easing** | **Auto**, **Linear**, **In**, **Out** or **In-out** |

Rows only appear when they apply. A spin has no intensity to tune, and a loop has no duration.

Every change replaces the motion you just picked rather than adding another block, and the whole pick and tune is one undo step.

The Tune panel also appears when you pick a preset from an empty **In**, **Emphasis** or **Out** slot in the timeline, from a block's **Change motion**, and from a track's right-click menu. A block changed with **Change motion** keeps its length, so its Duration row is hidden.

### Animate All With a Style Pack

To animate every layer in one click, use **Animate all** in the top bar. Pick a style pack:

| Pack | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Cascade** | Every layer rises in, top to bottom, 150ms apart |
| **Bloom** | Layers zoom in from the centre outward |
| **Punchy** | Headline stomps in, the rest bounce, CTA pulses at the end |
| **Elegant** | Slow fades and gentle rises, 300ms apart. Fits premium launches |
| **Minimal** | Only the headline and CTA rise in; everything else stays still |

It animates the layers you've selected, or every visible, unlocked layer when nothing is selected. frndOS picks the headline (the largest text) and the CTA (the smallest shape) for you.

A pack writes ordinary preset blocks, so you can drag, retime or swap any of them afterwards. Picking another pack replaces the previous pack's entrances instead of stacking on them. Your own emphasis, loops, exits and hand keyframes stay. One pack is one undo step.

### Previewing before you apply

Presets are shown as **cards that animate**. Hover one and it plays the motion in miniature, so you can tell Pop in from Slide in up without applying either.

> The preview is indicative — it shows the shape of the movement, not your layer at its real size or position.

### Staggering several layers

To bring layers in one after another instead of all at once: pick a preset from the **Preset…** dropdown in the top bar, then click **Stagger**. It applies that preset across your layers in sequence.

## Saved Motions — Reusing an Animation

Built a move you'll want again? Save it as a **template** and apply it to any layer, in any project on the same brand.

### Where the menu is

Every layer row in the left rail carries a **✨** button — _"Apply motion preset to &lt;layer&gt;"_. Right-clicking a timeline row opens the same panel.

It has three tabs:

| Tab | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **Default** | The preset library: **Entrance**, **Exit**, **Emphasis** and **Loop**, with the Tune panel after a pick |
| **Build** | Channel cards for composing your own motion |
| **Saved** | Your brand's saved motions |

> **Heads-up:** **Build** is offered on the layer rail's menu, not on a timeline segment's right-click menu.

### Composing your own with Build

The **Build** tab lets you pick what animates instead of taking a whole preset. Five channel cards are offered: **Path**, **Move**, **Scale**, **Rotate** and **Opacity**. Tick the ones you want, then click **Add at playhead**.

The panel describes what it writes: _"Pick what animates — a short highlight at the playhead that returns to where it started. Pull them apart in the layer's property lanes."_

> **Path and Move can't be picked together.** Both animate the same position, so ticking one unticks the other.

Picking a channel **adds** it to the block rather than replacing what's there, so you can layer a Scale onto a block that already moves without losing the movement.

A channel the block already animates is marked **Add another**: ticking it adds a second run of that effect after its last one (or at the playhead, when the playhead is past it). For an emphasis such as a spin, pulse, shake or blink, the second run repeats the motion rather than continuing it. If there's no room before the next block or the end of the clip, a toast says so.

> **Heads-up:** **Add at playhead** is disabled when the playhead is too close to the end — the reason reads _"Not enough room before the end."_

### Saving one

With a layer animated, open the **✨** menu and click **Save as template…**.

> **Heads-up:** the button is disabled on a layer with no animation yet — the tooltip says _"This layer has no animation yet."_

### Applying one

Open the **Saved** tab and click a card. Each card previews on hover like a built-in preset, and carries the duration it was built for — e.g. _"Built for 5s"_, with _"· by you"_ on your own.

> **It replaces the layer's whole animation.** The panel says so above the cards: _"Replaces this layer's whole animation."_

With nothing saved yet, the tab explains how to get there: _"No saved motions yet. Animate a layer, then save it as a template to reuse it."_

### Renaming and deleting

Hover a saved card in the **✨** menu and its rename and delete buttons appear.

> **Templates are brand-wide.** Everyone working on the same brand sees them — and deleting one takes it away from all of them. That's why the same library opens read-only from a timeline block's menu: deleting a brand template from behind a right-click on a block would be a trash icon in the wrong place.

> **A template can be applied to a project of a different length.** The duration on the card is information, not a restriction.

## The Timeline

The dock at the bottom carries the transport and the tracks.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Play / Pause** | Plays the timeline |
| **Loop** | Repeats playback |
| Time readout | Playhead position / total duration, plus the frame rate |
| **Zoom −/+/Fit** | How much timeline fits on screen — this changes the view only, not the video |
| **Slots** / **Keyframes** | Two ways to look at the same tracks |
| **Duration** | The video's length in seconds |

New projects start at **5 seconds** and **30 fps**.

### Slots vs Keyframes

**Slots** shows each layer's movement as blocks you can drag and stretch — the quick way to retime an entrance or an exit.

**Keyframes** shows the individual points, for when you want exact control. Insert, move, and delete keyframes here; the **Properties** panel on the right edits the selected one's values.

### Editing on the Canvas

You don't have to work in the timeline. Select a layer and you can **move, rotate and scale it directly on the canvas**; the keyframe is written where you drag, at the current playhead.

A badge on the focused tile tells you what the next change will write, before you make it:

| Badge | What your next move will do |
|---|---|
| **Base pose** | Set the layer's starting position, because the playhead is at 0 |
| **⏺ Keyframe 2.4s** | Rewrite the keyframe already sitting at that moment |
| **Keyframe @ 2.4s** | Create a new keyframe there |

> **Read the badge before you drag.** It's the difference between changing where a layer starts and adding a new moment mid-animation.

While a layer is selected you also get **ghosts** of its nearest keyframes either side of the playhead, and its motion path drawn on the canvas, so you can see where the layer is coming from and heading to. Both are hidden during playback, so what you watch matches the export exactly.

After a change at any point past the start, the editor plays the short span leading up to the playhead once, so you see the movement you just altered without reaching for Play.

### One row per property

A layer's track can be expanded into **one lane per property**, so a move and a fade stop being one block you can only drag together. Click the caret on the layer's name — _"Expand properties"_ / _"Collapse properties"_ — and the row splits into up to four lanes:

| Lane | Covers |
|---|---|
| **position** | Horizontal and vertical movement |
| **scale** | Size |
| **rotation** | Turning |
| **opacity** | Fading |

Each lane's bar drags and stretches on its own, so you can hold a layer's move where it is and let the fade run longer.

### Working with several bars at once

**⌘-click** bars to select several, on one layer or across layers. Dragging any selected bar moves all of them together, and stretching one stretches them together. **Delete** (or Backspace) removes them all in one undo step.

Right-click a selection for **Copy N segments** and **Delete N segments**. **⇧⌘C** copies the selection.

> **Copying works on one layer at a time.** A selection that spans layers can be moved and deleted, but not copied.

To paste, right-click a bar and choose **Paste N segments at the playhead**: the earliest segment lands at the playhead and the others keep their distance. Right-clicking an empty spot on a lane moves the playhead there and opens the layer's ✨ menu, which pastes the same way and also offers **At original time**, for keeping two layers in sync.

> **Why properties and not preset names:** a bar is labelled by what it actually animates. A preset name would become a lie the moment you hand-edit the keyframes; a property list stays true because it's read straight off them.

### Snapping

Dragging a bar **snaps** it to the meaningful moments on the timeline, and a guide line shows what it caught:

- The start and end of the video
- The **playhead**
- The preview-range brackets
- Another block's edge — on this layer or any other
- The audio clip's start or end
- A gridline on the ruler

A bar never snaps to its own current edges, and a real edge always wins over a gridline, so a tick can't steal a target you were actually aiming for. Snapping is measured in screen distance, so it feels the same at every zoom level.

## Bumpers — Reusing a Logo Sting

A **bumper** is a short clip or still that plays before or after your animation, typically a brand logo. Store it once and reuse it on any video instead of rebuilding it each time.

There are two slots: **Start bumper** and **End bumper**.

**Upload new** adds one to the library. Both images and video are accepted; a video brings its own natural length.

> **The library is brand-wide.** Everyone working on the same brand sees the same bumpers. That's the point: one approved sting, reused across every cut.

Each slot has its own settings:

| Setting | What it controls |
|---|---|
| **Duration in seconds** | How long the bumper holds |
| **Fit** | How the picture fills the frame |
| **Background colour** | What shows behind it |
| **Mute** and **Volume** | Audio, for a video bumper |

### Transitions

Each bumper carries a transition, named for the direction the picture travels: **Fade**, **Pop**, or a slide. A start bumper slides *from* a side (**Slide from left**, **Slide from top**), and an end bumper slides *to* one (**Slide to left**, **Slide to bottom**).

## Audio

The timeline has an audio row. Upload an `.mp3`, `.wav`, `.m4a`, or `.ogg` file, then adjust:

- **Volume**
- **Fade in duration**
- **Fade out duration**

Drag the audio block along the row to change when it starts. **Remove audio** takes it off again.

> **Heads-up:** there's a size limit — an oversized file is rejected with _"That audio file is too large."_

## Resyncing After Editing the KV

Changed the underlying KV after starting the motion? Open the **⋮** menu and choose **Resync from KV**.

The confirmation states exactly what happens: _"Tracks for overlays deleted in KV will be removed; keyframes for surviving overlays are kept."_

Resync keeps every size of a multi-size project. Each size picks up its latest finished artwork: sizes that follow a board take the board's current image, and sizes made from a resize take that resize's finished export.

## Commenting on a Clip

Feedback on a motion clip is pinned to a **moment**: every comment sits at a time on the timeline, which all sizes share. A comment can also say which size and which layer it's about.

### Turning comment mode on

Click the speech-bubble button at the left of the top-right pill (tooltip _"Comment on Motion (C)"_), or press **C**. The button shows how many comments are open. While comment mode is on, nothing on the canvas or the timeline can be dragged, so a click places feedback instead of moving something.

A **Comments** row appears under the timeline ruler, with the open comments as markers along it.

### Placing a comment

Where you click decides what the comment is about:

| Click | The comment is about |
|---|---|
| The **Comments** row or a track lane | The moment under the pointer, all sizes |
| A size on the canvas | That size and the layer under the pointer, at the playhead |
| A row in the layers rail | That layer, on the size you're editing, at the playhead |
| **Comment at playhead** on the Comments row (or **C**) | The playhead, all sizes |

Above the composer, tags show what you're commenting on:

* The time, for example **⏱ 00:07.24**. Click its **×** to make it a **Whole video** note instead; **Pin to** brings the time back
* The size: pick one, or **All sizes**
* The layer, if there is one. Click its **×** to drop it

Comments themselves work as everywhere else: reply, @mention, react, resolve (see [Commenting on the Canvas](./kv-generator#commenting-on-the-canvas)).

### Finding comments

Each size on the canvas shows how many comments are open on it. Click that count to open the newest one.

The comment dock has two filters, **All sizes** and **All layers**. They narrow the list, the markers and the timeline together, and dim the sizes you filtered out. Filtered to one size, the list splits into **… only** and **Also applies · all sizes**, because an all-size comment applies to that size too.

### Notes from the client

A client's notes on a shared or approval clip (see [Client Approvals](../collaboration/client-approvals#what-the-client-sees)) appear here too, as yellow numbered pins on that size around the moment they were left, and in the dock with the export they were left on, for example **Export v2**.

## Exporting

Click **Export** to open **Export motion** — _"Render this timeline to one MP4 per locked output size."_

1. Tick the sizes you want (or **Select all**)
2. Click **Export all**
3. Each row reports its own progress; a failed row can be retried on its own

Finished exports are reachable two ways: the **download icon** in the top bar (**Exported files**), and the exports toggle on the project's row back in the Motion projects panel.

> **Heads-up:** exporting needs browser codec support. If yours can't do it, the button is disabled with _"This browser can't export MP4 (missing codec support)."_

## Deleting a Motion Project

In the Motion projects panel, click the **trash** icon on a project row and confirm at **Delete this motion project?**

## FAQ

**Q: I want the fade to last longer than the move.**
Expand the layer's properties (the caret on its name) and drag the **opacity** lane on its own.

**Q: A bar keeps jumping to a position I didn't want.**
That's snapping catching a nearby guide — the playhead, another block's edge, the audio clip, or a gridline. Zoom in and the same pixel distance covers less time, so the pull is finer.

**Q: Can I reuse an animation on another layer or another project?**
Yes — save it via the layer's **✨** menu → **Save as template…**, then apply it from the **Saved** tab. Templates are shared across the brand.

**Q: I applied a saved motion and lost the animation the layer already had.**
Applying a saved motion replaces the layer's whole animation — the panel warns before you pick.

**Q: Someone deleted a saved motion I was using.**
Templates are brand-wide, so anyone on the brand can delete one. Already-applied keyframes stay on your layers; only the reusable card is gone.

**Q: I don't see the Motion tab.**
Motion is a Labs feature enabled per workspace. Ask an admin to turn it on — see [Turning On Experimental Features](../settings/experimental-features).

**Q: "New motion" is greyed out.**
There's no board to animate yet. Generate or open a board first — Motion animates the board you're viewing.

**Q: Motion says the file needs a brand.**
Motion works on brand-scoped KV files. Open the file inside a brand.

**Q: Can I have several motions for one KV?**
Yes. Each motion project is its own timeline document, listed under the Motion tab for that file.

**Q: I edited the KV after creating the motion. What now?**
Use **⋮ → Resync from KV**. Keyframes for layers that still exist are kept; tracks for deleted layers are removed.

**Q: Do I have to keyframe everything by hand?**
No. Apply a preset per layer with the **✨** button, use **Stagger** to sequence several layers at once, or **Animate all** to animate every layer with a style pack. Keyframe editing is there when you want exact control.

**Q: I picked a preset and tuned it several times. How do I undo it?**
One **Undo** takes back the pick and every tune after it.

**Q: Animate all picked the wrong layer as the headline.**
The headline is the largest text and the CTA is the smallest shape. Swap the motion on those layers afterwards: a pack's blocks are ordinary presets you can change.

**Q: Can I animate a Concept board?**
Motion animates the board you're viewing in the KV file. Promote a concept to Craft first — see [Craft Mode](./craft-mode).

**Q: What format does it export?**
MP4, one file per selected output size.

**Q: Where do I comment on a motion clip?**
Turn on comment mode with the speech-bubble button or **C**, then click the **Comments** row, a track, a size on the canvas, or a layer. See [Commenting on a Clip](#commenting-on-a-clip).

**Q: Export hung or failed in Safari.**
Fixed. Motion now exports in Safari. Reload the editor if it was open before the fix. If **Export** is greyed out with _"This browser can't export MP4 (missing codec support)"_, that browser can't encode video at all.

**Q: Do I need to save?**
No. The editor autosaves and shows **Saved** in the top bar. If it ever reads **Failed to save**, don't close the tab until it recovers.
