# Proposal Scorecard

**Proposal Scorecard** tests a proposal before it runs. A reviewer agent reads the proposal and scores it on two separate axes, **Fit** and **Ambition**, against the brand's approved [business priorities](../brand-setup/business-priorities) and its brand values. The two scores are never added together.

You can run it from the brand's **Research** pillar, or ask AskFRND to run one.

## Before You Start

1. ✅ **Scorecard access on your role** for this brand
2. ✅ **The proposal** — a PDF, PPTX, DOCX or TXT file, or its text to paste
3. ✅ **Credits** — a scoring run draws agent credits. Nothing is charged if the proposal is stopped before scoring
4. ✅ **Approved business priorities** (optional) — without them the proposal is scored on the brand's values only

## Step 1 — Start a Scorecard

1. Open the brand and go to **Research**
2. Click **New study**
3. Under **Or start from a method**, pick **Test a proposal before it runs** (**Proposal Scorecard**)

The page reads **Test a proposal before it runs**: _"A reviewer agent scores it on two separate axes — Fit and Ambition — against this brand's approved priorities and values. The two are never added together."_

At the top you see what the run will use, read-only:

* **Scorecard template** — the rubric (_"Change it in Settings → Scorecard Rubric."_)
* **Reviewer** — the proposal reviewer agent

## Step 2 — Add the Proposal

Under **What to score**, choose:

* **Upload a file** — **Choose a proposal — PDF, PPTX, DOCX or TXT**
* **Paste the text** — _"Paste the proposal — the objective, the idea, the KPIs and the plan."_ (up to 200,000 characters)

It's one or the other, not both.

Two optional fields add context:

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Business priority it ladders to** | Pick an approved priority, or leave it on **Detect it from the brief** so the reviewer suggests one |
| **Audience evidence** | Cite a persona study so the reviewer can weigh how the audience actually reacted |

The footer shows the credit estimate. Click **Score proposal**.

## Step 3 — Check the Brief

The reviewer first reads the proposal's own brief and KPIs and shows them to you: the **Campaign objective**, the **Campaign KPIs** (each with its **Type**, **Role**, **Baseline** and **Target**), and what it **Ladders to**. Click **Edit** to correct anything it misread, then **Done editing**.

Nothing is scored until you click **Looks right, score it**.

### When the proposal is stopped at the gate

A scorecard needs one outcome KPI with a baseline and a target. If the brief doesn't have one, or its main KPI is a media number such as reach, impressions or views, the run stops. The page shows **Stopped at the gate** and _"This proposal can't be scored yet"_, with a sentence naming what's missing.

**Edit the brief** to fix it, or **Send back to the team**. No credits are charged for a stop.

## Step 4 — Read the Scorecard

Scoring takes about a minute and a half. The scorecard then shows:

* **The verdict** in one sentence, for example _"Ready to run — Fit 78 clears the 65 floor."_ or _"Needs a revise — Fit 58 is below the 65 floor."_
* **Fit** and **Ambition** scores, with the Fit floor
* **The grid** — where the proposal lands: **Safe bet**, **Breakthrough**, **Stop** or **Wild**
* **What's stopping it** — a numbered list of what to fix, when something is
* **Recommendations** — numbered changes, each with **Read full recommendation**
* **Each criterion** with its score, the evidence behind it, and a confidence mark. A low-confidence score is marked `low · human call`
* **Analysis** — **What works**, **What weakens it**, **The unresolved trade-offs**, and whether the priority was matched

### Overriding a score

Disagree with a criterion? Click the pencil beside it, enter your score, and say _"What did you see that the reviewer didn't?"_ A reason is required. _"The reviewer's original score is kept beside yours."_ The totals are recalculated.

## Step 5 — Next Steps

The scorecard ends with actions:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Create tasks** | One per recommendation, assigned to the proposal owner in frndOS Tasks |
| **Re-score after revision** | Opens a fresh study that remembers this one, so the next scorecard shows what moved |
| **Test it with an audience** | Commissions a persona study on the same stimulus, as a draft. Launching it is your call |
| **Panel review** | Invites strategy, creative, media and account to check the scores. Each gets a task |
| **Export a deck** | The scorecard as slides, in Decks |
| **Client-ready summary** | A shareable document in Files, written from the scorecard |

## Running a Scorecard From AskFRND

Ask AskFRND to score a proposal, for example _"Score this proposal with a scorecard study"_ with the proposal attached or pasted. AskFRND starts the study and posts a **Proposal scorecard** card in the chat that fills in as the run progresses: the Fit and Ambition scores and the grid.

* **Open study** opens the full scorecard beside the chat (on a phone, it opens the study page)
* **Create tasks** creates the tasks right from the card

This uses the **Research studies** tool in **Chat Settings → Tools**: _"Lets AskFRND launch a Proposal Scorecard study on a brand's creative and read its findings."_ It's on by default; switch it off there if you don't want AskFRND to start studies. See [How AskFrnd Uses Tools](../askfrnd/tools).

## Setting the Rubric

The rubric decides what Fit and Ambition look at and how strict the gate is. It's set at two levels:

* **Workspace Settings → Scorecard Rubric** — _"The standard every brand in this workspace inherits. A brand can override any part of it."_
* **Brand Settings → Scorecard Rubric** — _"Only what this brand does differently. Anything you leave alone keeps following the workspace default."_

You can change:

* **Gates** — **Require a primary KPI**, **Require a baseline**, **Require a target**, **Reject a media-diagnostic primary KPI**
* **Fit floor** — a whole number from 0 to 100
* **Criteria** for each axis: name, weight and what the reviewer looks for
* **Ambition bands** — each band's name and range
* **Stakeholder lens**, **Version name**

Saving publishes a new version: _"New scorecards score against this version. Scorecards already run keep the version they used."_

## FAQ

**Q: Why aren't Fit and Ambition added into one score?**
They answer different questions. A proposal can fit the brief perfectly and take no risk, or be bold and miss the brief. A single total would hide which one it is.

**Q: My proposal was stopped at the gate. Was I charged?**
No. Stops are free. Fix the brief with **Edit the brief** and score it again.

**Q: Why was reach not accepted as the main KPI?**
Reach, impressions and views measure the media, not the business. The rubric can turn this rule off under **Gates**.

**Q: Can I pick a different rubric for one run?**
No. A run uses the brand's rubric (or the workspace's, if the brand hasn't changed it). Change it in **Scorecard Rubric** settings.

**Q: Does it need business priorities?**
No. Brands without approved priorities are still scored, on their brand values.

**Q: How long does scoring take?**
About a minute and a half after you confirm the brief.
