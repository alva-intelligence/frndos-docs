# Proposal Scorecard

**Proposal Scorecard** reviews the work before it runs. A reviewer agent reads the file, checks the brief, and scores it against the brand's approved [business priorities](../brand-setup/business-priorities), its brand values, and your rubric. It works on three kinds of work:

| Review type | What it covers | How it's scored |
|---|---|---|
| **Proposal** | Strategy and creative proposal decks | Two separate axes, **Strategy** and **Ambition**. They're never added together |
| **Creative material** | Storyboards, scripts, animatics and key visuals | Each dimension 0–100, weighted into one **Overall**, plus mandatory checks |
| **Media plan** | Media, channel and KOL plans | Each dimension 0–100, weighted into one **Overall**, plus mandatory checks |

You can run it from the brand's **Research** pillar, or ask AskFRND to run one.

When the work is scored, you can share it as a full report, a PDF or slides. See [Scorecard Reports and Rubrics](./scorecard-report-and-rubric).

## Before You Start

1. ✅ **Scorecard access on your role** for this brand
2. ✅ **The work to score**: a PDF, PPTX, DOCX or TXT file, or its text to paste
3. ✅ **Credits.** Scoring draws credits. Nothing is charged if the work is stopped before scoring
4. ✅ **Approved business priorities** (optional). Without them, the work is scored on the brand's values only

## Step 1 — Start a Scorecard

Start from any of these:

* On the brand's **Research** Overview, pick **Test a proposal before it runs** under **Or choose a method**
* Drop or attach a proposal on the Research question box and click **Review it**
* Drop a deck or document anywhere in the brand and pick **Review this proposal**

Starting from a dropped file brings the file with it into Step 2.

## Step 2 — Add the Work

The setup is titled **Review a proposal**: _"Add the file. We check it against your brand and tell you what to fix."_

Drop the file on **Drop a proposal, deck or creative**, or click **Upload a file**. To score text instead, choose **or paste the text** (up to 200,000 characters). It's one or the other, not both. Once a file is in, its card offers **Replace** and **Remove**.

**What it is** sets the review type: **Let us check**, **Proposal**, **Creative material** or **Media plan**. With **Let us check**, the reviewer works out the type and you confirm it with the brief.

A line shows what the review is judged against: the rubric, the brand's Brand IQ, and the priority if one is pinned. **See how it's judged** opens a side panel with the rubric's criteria, the brand's approved priorities, its Brand IQ and any audience evidence, with links to change each one if you're allowed.

**Add context (optional)** folds away two fields:

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Business priority** | The priority this should serve. Leave it on **Let the reviewer pick** and the reviewer suggests one |
| **Audience evidence** | Cite a persona study so the reviewer can weigh how the audience actually reacted |

The footer shows how long it takes and the estimated credits, for example **About 3 min**, and says _"You're only charged if it gets scored."_ Click **Score it** (with a type picked, the button names it, for example **Score proposal**).

## Step 3 — Confirm the Parts (Mixed Files)

Some files hold more than one piece of work, for example a campaign proposal with its video storyboard at the back. The reviewer finds the parts and asks you to confirm them before anything is scored.

The screen reads **This file has two parts** (or three, or four) with a strip of the file's pages coloured by part. Each part shows its name, its review type, its pages, and what the reviewer found there.

* **Adjust pages** lets you rename a part, change its review type, or move a page boundary. Click **Done adjusting** when you're finished
* **Add a part** covers pages no part has yet. Pages no part covers are listed as not scored
* **Score as one** scores the whole file as a single piece of work instead

Click **Score N parts · X credits**. Each part is scored with its own rubric, side by side. A file can have up to four parts, and parts can't share pages.

A file with a single piece of work skips this step.

## Step 4 — Check the Brief

The reviewer first reads the brief and shows it to you. For a proposal, that's the **Campaign objective**, the **Campaign KPIs** (each with its **Type**, **Baseline**, **Target** and **Role**), and what it **Ladders to**. For a creative or a media plan, it also reads details such as format, mandatories, budget, channels and markets.

The **Review type** sits at the top. If the reviewer wasn't sure, it says so and asks you to pick. Change it if it's wrong, and the brief below adapts.

Click **Edit** to correct anything the reviewer misread, then **Done editing**. Nothing is scored until you confirm.

### When the proposal is stopped at the gate

A proposal needs one outcome KPI with a baseline and a target. If the brief doesn't have one, or its main KPI is a media number such as reach, impressions or views, the run stops at the gate. No credits are charged for a stop. Fix the brief and confirm again.

## Step 5 — Read the Scorecard

Scoring usually takes about three minutes. The page shows each step as it runs: **Read the file and the brief**, **Score it against the rubric**, and **A second look if it lands near a band line**. You can leave: if you launched the scorecard, you're notified when it's ready (_"You'll be notified when it's ready"_). Click **Turn off** if you don't want that, or **Notify me when it's ready** to turn it back on.

> **Rolling out:** the answer-first scorecard described here may reach workspaces gradually. If yours looks different, refresh the page.

The scorecard is one page that starts with the verdict.

### The answer

The top card says in one sentence how the work stands, with:

* **A creative or a media plan:** the **Overall** score out of 100 and its band: **Ready** (85+), **Nearly ready** (70–84), **Needs work** (50–69) or **Not ready** (below 50)
* **A proposal:** where it lands on the **Strategy** and **Ambition** grid (**Safe Bet**, **Breakthrough**, **Stop** or **Wild**), with each score and its line. Strategy and Ambition are never added together

Beside the score you may see **Close to the line**, when it's within 3 points of a band so a small change could move it, the change **since the last review** on a re-score, and about where it would land **after the changes**.

Under it, **Judged against** names the rubric, Brand IQ and priority this run used. **How this was judged** opens the same panel as in setup, frozen to what this run used.

The **Ask FRND** chips rewrite the scorecard's words: **Shorter**, **For the CMO**, **Bahasa Indonesia** or **More formal**. Scores don't change.

### What to fix first

The fixes, in priority order. Each one says who makes it (**Agency** or **Brand**), which pages it's about, and what it should gain.

Click **Create N tasks** to turn the fixes into [Tasks](../workspace/tasks). Each fix gets a suggested owner, and you can pick someone else before creating them. A fix with a task then shows **In Tasks** or **Done**, with the assignee. Tick a fix here to close its task, or close it in Tasks: the two always agree.

When the last fix is done, whoever created the tasks gets a notification: _"Every fix is done. Upload the revised file to re-score it."_

### The rest of the page

| Section | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **What's working** | The parts that already work |
| **Scores** | Each criterion or dimension with its score. An overridden score shows what the reviewer gave and why it was changed |
| **Checks** | Mandatory checks, each **Clear** or **Flag** (for example **1 of 4 flagged**, or **All clear**). Checks are never scored or averaged in |
| **Work on it in frndOS** | _"Test it, get the team's view, or re-score a revised file."_ See [Step 6](#step-6--next-steps) |
| **How it was scored** | The method, the bands, and whether it was scored more than once because it landed near a band line |

Page references in the scorecard open the page of the file they came from.

### Sharing and exporting

**Share** and **Export** sit in the scorecard's header. **Export** offers **Summary PDF**, **Full review PDF**, **Slides**, **Client summary**, and **Copy the fixes**, which copies the fixes as text to paste into an email or a Lark message. See [Scorecard Reports and Rubrics](./scorecard-report-and-rubric).

### Overriding a score

Disagree with a score? Click the pencil beside it, enter your score, and say _"What did you see that the reviewer didn't?"_ _"The reviewer's original score is kept beside yours."_ The totals are recalculated.

## Reading a File Scored in Parts

A file scored in parts opens on one answer across all of them, headed **Scorecard · N parts**:

* **Overall readiness** is set by the weakest part, and says which part that is. Parts are never blended into one number
* **A card per part** shows its score (Strategy and Ambition for a proposal, or Overall for a creative or plan), its band, and its checks. Click a card to open that part's full scorecard

_"Each part keeps its full scorecard: the matrix or bars, checks, page evidence and overrides. Re-scoring a part re-runs that part only."_

### Re-scoring one part

Revised one part of the file? Click **Re-score this part** on its card. Choose to upload the revised file (say which pages are this part, or tick **The whole file is this part**), or **Re-score the same pages**. Click **Re-score · N credits**. The other parts stay as they are.

## Step 6 — Next Steps

**Work on it in frndOS** offers:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Re-score after revision** | Opens a fresh study that remembers this one, so the next scorecard shows what moved |
| **Test it with an audience** | Commissions a persona study on the same stimulus, as a draft. Launching it is your call |
| **Panel review** | Invites strategy, creative, media and account to check the scores. Each gets a task |

Tasks for the fixes are made from **What to fix first**, and the client summary from **Export**.

## Running a Scorecard From AskFRND

Ask AskFRND to score a proposal, for example _"Score this proposal with a scorecard study"_, with the file attached or the text pasted. AskFRND starts the study and posts a scorecard card in the chat that fills in as the run progresses.

* **Open study** opens the full scorecard beside the chat (on a phone, it opens the study page)
* **Create tasks** creates the tasks right from the card

This uses the **Research studies** tool in **Chat Settings → Tools**. If it's off, AskFRND offers to turn it on for you (see [How AskFrnd Uses Tools](../askfrnd/tools#turning-a-tool-on-from-the-chat)).

## FAQ

**Q: Why aren't Strategy and Ambition added into one score?**
They answer different questions. A proposal can fit the brief perfectly and take no risk, or be bold and miss the brief. A single total would hide which one it is.

**Q: My file has a proposal and a storyboard. Do I need two studies?**
No. The reviewer finds the parts and scores each one with its own rubric. Or click **Score as one** to score the whole file together.

**Q: Why is the overall readiness so low when most parts scored well?**
Readiness follows the weakest part, so one part needing rework holds the whole file back. Open that part's card to see why.

**Q: My older scorecards said Minor revision, Major revision or Rework. Where did those go?**
The bands were renamed. **Minor revision** is now **Nearly ready**, **Major revision** is **Needs work**, and **Rework** is **Not ready**. Older scorecards show the new names too, on the scorecard, in chat and in exports. The score ranges didn't change, and a band your rubric gave its own name keeps that name.

**Q: My proposal was stopped at the gate. Was I charged?**
No. Stops are free. Fix the brief and score it again.

**Q: Why was reach not accepted as the main KPI?**
Reach, impressions and views measure the media, not the business. The rubric can turn this rule off.

**Q: Can I pick a different rubric for one run?**
No. A run uses the brand's rubric (or the workspace's, if the brand hasn't changed it). See [Setting the Rubric](./scorecard-report-and-rubric#setting-the-rubric).

**Q: Does it need business priorities?**
No. Brands without approved priorities are still scored, on their brand values.

**Q: If I tick a fix done on the scorecard, does its task close too?**
Yes. A fix and its task always agree, whichever one you tick.

**Q: Will the Ask FRND rewrite change my scores?**
No. It only rewrites the words. The scores stay as they were.
